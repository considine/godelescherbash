Getting here

The Bash takes place on MIT's main campus in Cambridge, a few minutes from Kendall Square and across the river from Boston.

✈ By air

Boston Logan (BOS) is 20 minutes away by car or about 35 minutes by the Silver Line and Red Line to Kendall/MIT. April flights tend to sell out, so book early.

🚆 By rail

Amtrak goes to Boston South Station. From there, the Red Line runs to Kendall/MIT in about 10 minutes.

🏨 Lodging

Discounted room blocks are held at partner hotels in Kendall Square and Central Square. Registered guests get booking links by email.

The Experience

Three rooms. One braid.

Each of the book's three threads gets its own installation, built by artists, engineers and musicians from around Cambridge. Guests move through all three over the weekend on timed entries.

G

The Gödel Room

A 60-minute escape room built around self-reference and formal systems. Teams of up to six work through a sequence of logic puzzles based on the MIU system, Gödel numbering and the liar paradox, and the last lock opens only if they've built the room's own key from inside it. Designed with puzzle makers from the MIT Mystery Hunt community.

Teams of 4–6 · 60 min · Booking requiredStata Center · R&D Commons, 4th Floor
E

Escher Hall

A walk-through installation of projection-mapped architecture. Staircases rise without end, tessellations shift and grow across the floor, and a nine-meter version of Drawing Hands is drawn live, line by line, over the course of the weekend.

Timed entry · 25 minBuilding 10 · Lobby 10 & the Great Dome
B

The Bach Chamber

A spatial-audio performance of the Musical Offering on a 64-speaker array. The Crab Canon is staged so guests who enter from opposite doors hear the same piece, one forward and one in reverse, and the two meet in the middle of the room.

Performances hourlyKresge Auditorium

The opening keynote has become the Bash's signature moment. This year it premieres as a full-length film, followed by a live conversation with the speaker in Kresge Auditorium.

“Finding Strange Loops in Strange Places”

World premiere · Friday, April 16 · 7:00 PM ET

Hofstadter described a strange loop as a path through a hierarchy that ends up back where it began. He found them in Gödel's proofs, Escher's lithographs and Bach's canons. Dr. Vale argues that they turn up in much less likely places too: in immune systems, financial markets, language models, family traditions and the way a city recognizes itself. The keynote traces how self-reference becomes selfhood, and why that matters more now than it did in 1979.

FAQ

Questions

Do I need to have read Gödel, Escher, Bach?

No. Plenty of guests come having read a few chapters, or none. Every talk is pitched at a general audience, and the read-aloud is a good way to get through the book.

How does the Gödel Room escape room work?

Teams of 4–6 get 60 minutes. You can book a team slot when you pick up your badge or join a mixed team on the spot. No background in math is needed; in 2026, about 34% of teams escaped.

Can I come and go during the 24-hour read-aloud?

Yes. General admission guests can come and go freely. VIP front-row seats are assigned, and VIP guests are expected to attend.

Is the afterparty included?

Weekend and VIP passes include the afterparty. It's 21+, so bring ID along with your badge.

Why “Bash”?

Because Bach would have thrown one. The Bash started in 2025 as a small dinner party for people who had read the book too many times, and it grew from there.

Accessibility

All venues are wheelchair accessible. Escher Hall offers a low-motion mode, and the Bach Chamber has assisted-listening devices. Email the access team if you need anything else.
